Controlled change
-----------------
Precompute previous/next wrapped indices for every x and y coordinate once in
the Life constructor, then reuse them inside the existing nested neighbour
loop. The two-dimensional byte grid, two-buffer update, Life rule, traversal,
compiler flags, and benchmark interface remain unchanged.

Hypothesis
----------
Removing repeated wrapped coordinate/modulo calculations from the measured
96.61% V0 neighbour-calculation hotspot may reduce instruction overhead. This
does not claim a cache or memory-bandwidth improvement.

Formal Rangpur benchmark
-------------------------
Slurm Job 562440 completed 0:0 on a100-0. It used the same formal matrix and
resources as V0: 512, 1024, and 2048 square grids; 800 generations; density
35%; seed 12345; five repetitions; one node/task/CPU; GCC 8.5.0; and the same
`-O3 -DNDEBUG` benchmark flags. Every V1 live-cell count and checksum matched
the corresponding V0 result.

Median V1 runtimes were 1.276579850 s, 5.069925500 s, and 20.354371500 s.
Against the V0 medians, these are speedups of 4.465737x, 4.490008x, and
4.463521x, or runtime reductions of 77.607278%, 77.728327%, and 77.596164%.
V1 coefficients of variation were 0.279608%, 0.126138%, and 0.093286%.

Profiling
---------
Slurm Job 562441 completed 0:0 on a100-0 using the same V0 profile workload
and `-O2 -g -pg` flags. V1 produced the same live-cell count and checksum as
V0. In V1, gprof attributed 75.02% of sampled self time to
`Life::live_neighbours` and 22.94% to `Life::step`, compared with 96.61% and
3.54% respectively in V0. Profile-build wall time fell from 207.768831 s to
92.7033395 s, but profile timings are diagnostic and are not substituted for
the formal `-O3` benchmark.

Decision
--------
Retain V1. The large, consistent formal speedup supports the hypothesis that
repeated wrapped-index computation was a major part of the V0 hotspot. The
remaining work is still dominated by cell stepping and neighbour access.
